How to hear your screenplay read out loud: a table read on iPhone

Some lines look fine on the page and sound wrong out loud. A table read is how you find them, and your iPhone can run one any time, with a voice for every character. Here is how to start it, how to get better voices for free, what to listen for, and how to read a part yourself.

Hear your script read out loud

Dialogue is written to be heard, and the page hides a lot: a line nobody would say, a speech that runs out of breath, two characters who sound the same. Reading silently, you skim past them. A table read, where the script is read out loud from start to finish, is where they show up.

Scenarist can read your script to you on your iPhone, line by line, and with Scenarist Pro every character speaks in a voice of their own. This lesson reads Booth Three, a new scene for Ruth and Dev from lesson 6, with a third voice: Otis, the night guard.

Action

Open Read Aloud

Open the script, then:

  1. Tap the ••• menu.
  2. Tap Export & Settings, then Read Aloud.
  3. Press play.

The narrator reads the scene headings and the action, and the line being read lights up, with the script scrolling along so you can follow it. The arrows step back or ahead a line, the slider under them sets the speed, from the tortoise to the hare, and the gear holds the speed and pitch for the whole read.

On the free plan, the narrator reads every part, and says who’s speaking before each line: “Otis says: Done already? You two went in an hour ago.” That’s already enough to hear how a scene moves.

Character

A voice for every character

With Scenarist Pro, every character gets their own voice, and the narrator keeps the headings and the action:

  1. In Read Aloud, tap the gear and turn on Character Voices.
  2. Tap the masks to see your cast: the narrator, then every character with a line in the script.
  3. Tap a name, then a voice. You hear it right away: “This is how Dev will sound.”

Scenarist casts the script for you, so you only change the parts you want to. A voice that another character already has is marked in the list, like Also Otis under Tom. Above the voices, each character has a pitch and a speed of their own. They work on top of the main sliders, so those still move everyone together.

Action

Better voices, free

Out of the box, your iPhone’s voices can sound robotic. Apple’s better ones, marked Enhanced and Premium, are a free download:

  1. Open Settings and search for “Voices”. It’s under Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices.
  2. Tap English, then the download button next to a few Enhanced or Premium voices.

They’re big downloads, a few hundred megabytes each, so use Wi-Fi. Once they’re on your iPhone, they’re in Scenarist’s voice lists too, marked Enhanced or Premium. To hear the difference, the video plays Ruth’s line twice: in a basic voice, then in Ava (Premium).

Dialogue

What to listen for

Now listen like an audience. Three things are easy to skim past on the page and hard to miss out loud:

What you hearWhat to try
A line nobody would really sayWrite what the character would say. If a line is only there to tell the audience something, find another way in.
A speech too long for one breathCut it, or break it up with an action line or the other character’s reply.
Characters who all talk the same wayGive each one their own words and rhythm. Cover the names: you should still know who’s speaking.

Dev’s first line has the first two at once.

Dialogue

Fix a line, hear it again

Here’s Dev’s line as it was written:

Booth Three · Before

DEV

As you know, Ruth, the Night Desk has gone out from booth three every night for twenty-two years, and nobody has ever gone in there after we sign off.

Nobody says “as you know” to someone who already knows. Ruth doesn’t need telling, so the line is really talking to the audience, and read out loud, it runs out of breath before it ends.

Cut it to what Dev would really say:

Booth Three · After

DEV

Twenty-two years. Nobody goes in there after we sign off.

Then play it again. The audience still learns how long it’s been, and now it sounds like someone talking.

Character

Read a part yourself

To read a part yourself, mute that character: tap the masks, then the speaker next to the name. The row now says You read this part.

When the read gets to that part, the narrator still says the name, then waits about as long as the line takes, so you can say it. Under the controls, it says whose line it is: Ruth — your line. Tap the forward arrow to move on sooner.

It’s a scene partner for rehearsing, any time. To give everyone their voice back, choose Unmute All in the cast’s ••• menu.

A voice for every character, and muting a part, are part of Scenarist Pro. Read Aloud with the narrator is free.

How the scene ends

Here is Booth Three as it plays at the end of the lesson, with Dev’s line cut: a narrator, three voices, and well under a page.

Booth Three

FADE IN:

INT. RADIO STATION - NIGHT

The hall is dark, except for the ON AIR light over booth three. Ruth and Dev stop dead.

DEV

Twenty-two years. Nobody goes in there after we sign off.

OTIS (70s), the night guard, shuffles up with a flashlight.

OTIS

Done already? You two went in an hour ago.

RUTH

An hour ago, we were at my place.

OTIS

Then who signed the book?

He holds it up. Two signatures, an hour old: hers, and Dev's.

Through the glass, a tape reel turns. The label, in Ruth's handwriting: TOMORROW.

RUTH

That's my writing.

DEV

Then play it.

She pushes the fader up. Her own voice fills the hall.

RUTH (V.O.)

Good evening. This is the last Night Desk.

FADE OUT.
