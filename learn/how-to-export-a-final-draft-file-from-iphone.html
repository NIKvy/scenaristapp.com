How to export a Final Draft (.fdx) file from your iPhone

Your script is on your iPhone, but the people you work with use Final Draft. Send them an .fdx file and it opens with every line formatted, ready to edit. Here is where the export is, how to get the file where it’s going, what comes across, and how to open one someone sends back.

Send a Final Draft file

A draft written on a phone rarely stays there. It goes to a co-writer, a producer, a director, or your own Mac, and on the other end it’s usually opened in Final Draft. This lesson sends Two Mugs, Ruth and Dev’s scene from lesson 6, from an iPhone as a Final Draft file, then opens one that comes back.

Character

PDF or FDX?

First, which file. A PDF is for reading: it looks the same on every screen and prints the same everywhere, but nobody can edit it as a script. An FDX, Final Draft’s own file, is for writing. It opens in Final Draft, and in most screenwriting apps, as a script someone can keep working on.

PDFFDX
It’s forReadingWriting
Opens inAnything that shows a PDFFinal Draft, and most screenwriting apps
Can be editedNot as a scriptYes, line by line
Send it toReaders, contests, actorsCo-writers, producers, your own Mac

So the question is what happens next. If they’ll read it, send the PDF. If they’ll work on it, send the FDX.

Export: Final Draft (.fdx)

Open the script, then:

  1. Tap the ••• menu.
  2. Tap Export & Settings, then Export. Every format Scenarist makes is on this screen.
  3. Under Other Formats, tap Final Draft (.fdx).

The file is ready in a moment, named after the script: Two Mugs.fdx.

Final Draft export is part of Scenarist Pro. PDF, Fountain and plain text export are free.

Action

AirDrop, Mail or Files

Now choose where it goes. AirDrop it to your Mac, attach it to an email or a message, or tap Save to Files. Saved in iCloud Drive, it’s on your computer a moment later. Open it in Final Draft, and keep writing.

Character

What comes across

Everything that makes it a script. Each line keeps its element: scene headings, action, character names, dialogue, parentheticals and transitions, so Final Draft knows a character name from a line of action. Dual dialogue stays side by side, and the title page comes too. If you’re tracking revisions, the changed lines keep their marks.

In the scriptIn the .fdx
Scene headings, action, characters, dialogue, parentheticals, transitionsYes, each as its own element
Dual dialogueYes, side by side
Title pageYes
Revision marksYes, on the changed lines, with the revision’s colour in the header
Page breaks, and act breaks in a TV scriptYes
Scene numbersNo: number the scenes in Final Draft
Notes and highlightsNo: they stay in Scenarist

Export from the library

In a hurry? You don’t have to open the script. In the library, touch and hold it and choose Export. It’s the same screen, with the same Final Draft button.

Scene heading

Import an FDX

It works the other way, too. Got an .fdx from someone? In the library, tap +, then Import Script, and choose the file. Before anything opens, Scenarist shows you what’s inside: that it’s a Final Draft file, how many elements, scenes and characters it has, and its first lines. Import it, and it opens as a script you can keep writing.

Fountain files (.fountain or .txt) import the same way, and importing is free. Fountain is a plain-text screenplay format that many screenwriting apps can save.
